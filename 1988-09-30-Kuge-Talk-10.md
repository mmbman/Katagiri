#### 0:00

14th line from the top at page 68:

> The Buddhas, enlightened ones, cultivating these *flowers in the sky*, thereby *acquire the robe, seat, and room*, attain enlightenment and realize its fruition. *Holding up a flower and winking* is a public case where *cataracts in the eye*, *flowers in the sky* manifest. The *treasury of the eye of true teaching, the ineffable mind of nirvana*, correctly transmitted to the present without lapse, is called *cataracts in the eye, flowers in the sky*. Enlightenment, nirvana, the body of reality, inherent nature, and so on are a few petals of the *opening five petals* of *flowers in the sky*.
>
> – From *Shōbōgenzō: Zen Essays by Dōgen*, translated by Thomas Cleary.

Yesterday I [talked] a little bit about the *acquiring the robe, seat and room*, which occurs in the *Saddharma Pundarika Sutra*. Let me read that part. I think you will see this paragraph in *The Threefold Lotus Sutra*, page 191 to 192:

> "Medicine King! If there be any good son or good daughter who after the
extinction of the Tathagata desires to preach this Law-Flower Sutra to the four groups,
how should he preach it? That good son or good daughter, entering into the abode of
the Tathagata, wearing the robe of the Tathagata, and sitting on the throne of the
Tathagata, should then widely proclaim this sutra to the four groups [of hearers].
>
> "The abode of the Tathagata is the great compassionate heart within all living
beings; the robe of the Tathagata is the gentle and forbearing heart; the throne of the
Tathagata is the voidness of all laws. Established in these, then with unflagging mind
to bodhisattvas and the four groups [of hearers] he will preach this Law-Flower Sutra.”
>
> – From *The Threefold Lotus Sutra*, translated by Bunno Kato.

The Lotus Sutra says like this. 

So, I think by the practicing cultivating of flowers in the sky, which means everything you can see, you can hear, is nothing but total the manifestation of the Buddha's world, Buddha's heart. So, nothing to waste, nothing to throw away, nothing to attach to for long, for your own. So, that is [that] then you can get the robe and seat and room; that means you can get *great compassion*, and *gentle and forbearing heart*, and also *emptiness of all things*. 

The compassion is the Buddha's total personality. Buddha cannot enter into the compassionate world, cannot leave the compassionate world. It means that Buddha's whole body and mind is already compassion; exactly compassion. In other words, *produced by compassion*. So that is the great compassion that the Buddha and bodhisattva possess. 

So, we are already, our body and mind is nothing but compassion that everyone has. So your human body, the human body is very precious for us. 

Then, naturally we can practice gentle and forbearing, you can get the patience, and gentleness and kindness, compassionate mind, softness. That means that you can see the depths of human existence, and it touches the core of all sentient beings. And then, naturally you can open your heart to all sentient beings. That is called *gentleness*; *softness*. Without creating *arrogance*. 

So, constantly, whatever happens, that gentleness, compassion is sharing with all sentient beings, under all circumstances. This is *wearing the robe*. Then, naturally you can get this one. 

And the *emptiness of all things* means *the practice of egolessness*. 

The practice of egolessness: in the *Blue Cliff Record*, case number 2, page 10, I think you will see this term, *trailing mud and dripping water*. It says,

> To say the word “Buddha” is trailing mud and dripping water; ...

*Trailing mud* means you can get your body completely wet by the mud. And pouring the water on the top of your head, and completely you can get your body wet. It means you have to teach the Buddha's teaching according to the circumstances of each personality. That is a teaching, teaching the Buddha's teaching, working with all sentient beings hand in hand. That is, the *Blue Cliff Record* uses the technical term, *trailing mud and dripping water*. This is a practice of *egolessness*. 

That's why we practice together like this. If you live alone, I think it seems to be easy, but if you live together like this, it's very difficult. Because you have to teach the truth according to the different circumstances, different personalities. It's not so easy to practice together like this. That's why lots of complaints come up. And if you are really involved in complaining too much, you completely forget the main point, what the practice is. 

So I think through and through we have to practice egolessness. Egolessness is no arrogance. So very naturally you can bow fully, prostrating your body and mind in the presence of Buddha. You can do it. 

That is, and then this says, “thereby *acquire the robe, seat, and room*, attain enlightenment and realize its fruition.” 

So very naturally, what is enlightenment? What did Buddha try to teach us? What should we learn from the Buddha’s teaching? Very naturally you can attain enlightenment and realize its fruition. 

So, “acquire the robe and seat and room” – compassion, patience, and emptiness – is simultaneously fruit, enlightenment. 

So day by day all we have to do is to cultivate the flowers in the sky. That means every fruit; we have to deal with each single aspect of everyday living with compassion and emptiness and gentleness, and with patience. And *then* you attain enlightenment, which makes you feel relief completely. That is “attain enlightenment and realize its fruition.” 

So *fruition* is [constant] continuation of practicing, cultivating the flowers in the sky. When you see the table, you have to cultivate the flower in the sky. *Flower in the sky* means the form of the table is the flower in the sky you can see with your eyes. *Any* form: the form of a table, form of the floor, form of a flower, form of an insect, all things. 

So *cultivating the flower in the sky* means handle with compassion, patience, and emptiness. Empty heart; then this is simultaneously attaining enlightenment continually. This is a *fruition*. 

#### 13:44

> *Holding up a flower and winking* is a public case where *cataracts in the eye*, *flowers in the sky* manifest. 

Of course, the udumbara flowers are not merely the flowers as an object you can see. But this flower, udumbara flower, talks a lot about human life, about the human world, based on the truth. That is why the udumbara flower is already talking [about] flowers in the sky. 

So that’s why by seeing the [Buddha] holding up a flower, Mahakasyapa can attain enlightenment. 

And then *winking* is also nothing but flowers in the sky. Because winking is not winking; winking is winking to the universe. So very naturally through the winking you can see the whole world, the manifestation of the whole world. That is why here it says, “*cataracts in the eye*, *flowers in the sky* manifest.”

#### 15:10

> The *treasury of the eye of true teaching, the ineffable mind of nirvana*, correctly transmitted to the present without lapse, is called *cataracts in the eye, flowers in the sky*. 

 The *treasury of the eye of true teaching* is called *shōbōgenzō* in Japanese. *Shōbōgenzō*, *the ineffable mind of Nirvana*: ineffable mind is nirvana; nirvana is ineffable mind. Because nirvana is not a dead, dreary state of human life, but it’s alive in every aspect of human life. Which is subtle, which is ineffable, which is unexplainable, but it’s working. That is why “ineffable mind of nirvana.” 
 
“Correctly transmitted to the present without lapse”: Now Shakyamuni Buddha told to Mahakasyapa, I can transmit this *shōbōgenzō*, the ineffable mind of nirvana, to you now, so from now on you should keep it for long, without lapse. [This] is called *cataracts in the eyes, flowers in the sky*. This is called *flowers in the sky*. 

Shakyamuni Buddha holding up a flower and winking is not merely legendary stories. This is talking about human life: how rare it is, how precious it is, how sublime human life is. So that’s why through your human body you can notice holding up the udumbata flower and winking. You can see how sublime human life is, how rare human body and mind is. 

#### 17:33

> Enlightenment, nirvana, the body of reality, inherent nature, and so on are a few petals of the *opening five petals* of *flowers in the sky*.

So *enlightenment, nirvana, the body of reality* means dharma body, *dharmakaya*. Enlightenment, nirvana, dharmakaya, inherent nature means the ultimate nature of existence, which are invisible. They are also nothing but the flowers in the sky. 

So you don’t like thought, you don’t like thinking, ideas, but ideas and thoughts are very important for us. If you use your ideas and thoughts just in your own territory, *then* ideas become egoistic, selfish. But if you use ideas and thoughts for all sentient beings, for helping all beings, then it really works, because the ideas come from life, ideas come from flowers in the sky, ideas come from the truth as it is. So the ideas have lots of words, lots of heart, lots of spirit, with human warmness. That’s why ideas and thoughts are very important for us. 

That’s why if you have a certain job, you cannot help thinking, about ideas, for helping all sentient beings, wherever you may be. 

So that’s why here it says, all things invisible are also very important, which is called flowers in the sky. Even though it is a few petals. 

The opening of five petals means even little ideas. For instance, if you start to have this kind of training or sessions, and you have a particular way to become a director or *ino*, and if you have a certain position as a work leader, you have to have lots of tiny ideas, lots of tiny things you have to think. You cannot ignore it. But these tiny ideas, little thoughts, few petals, are important for us. 

Sometimes a few ideas, little ideas are not important, so you are not necessary to think carefully. But it is, that is, even though you don’t think carefully, it is important, ok? It *is* important. 

So that’s why here it says, even a few petals of the opening five petals of flowers in the sky are also very important for us. 

#### 21:23

> Shakyamuni Buddha said, “It is like someone with cataracts seeing flowers in the sky: when the affliction of cataracts is removed, the flowers perish in the sky.”

Dōgen Zenji commented on Shakyamuni Buddha’s statement. Shakyamuni Buddha said, “it is like someone with catalogs seeing flowers in the sky,” because a person who can see the flowers in the sky is a sign of sickness of his eyes with cataracts. And then when you recover from that sickness, then the flowers are gone; because the flowers are actually not existing beings. So, that’s why if you recover from sickness or if you go beyond hallucinations, et cetera, you can see all flowers, all imaginations are gone. And then, “affliction of cataracts is removed, the flowers perish in the sky.” Then you are free from suffering, et cetera. That is the usual sense, the usual meaning of Shakyamuni Buddha. 

But Dōgen Zenji comments on that like this: 

> There has not yet been a scholar who has understood this statement. Because they don’t know *the sky*, they don’t know the *flowers in the sky*. Because they don’t know the *flowers in the sky*, they don’t know *the person with cataracts*; they do not see *the person with cataracts*, do not meet *the person with cataracts*, are not *the person with
cataracts*. 

Because if you understand the Buddha’s statement in the usual sense, I think you are a person who doesn’t understand exactly *flowers in the sky*, you don’t know the sky, you don’t know the person who has eyes with cataracts, et cetera. He mentioned like that. 

So, “because they don’t know the sky” – later Dōgen Zenji talks about the sky, so, let’s go.

They don’t know the sky, they don’t know the flowers in the sky. Because they don’t know the flowers in the sky, they don’t know the person with cataracts. 

Usually we are a person with cataracts, because we have a consciousness. Consciousness is already perverted, that means already moved. Perverted views, creation of perverted views by consciousness means consciousness already has been moved, vibrated – vibration of consciousness. That is called not seeing the things as they really are. So, we call them *perverted* views. So, from this point, we are already sick, you know? *[He laughs.]* So, we don’t see the actual, real things. Something like that. 

So, from this point, consciousness, if you see the flower of the table, in other words the form of a table, that is already the form of a table reflected in your eyes. Okay? In your eyes. So, what is this? This is already the perverted view, according to the Buddha’s excellent skillful means. Then, we are a perverted person, and the table is the two beings – so separated. Okay? So at that time we don’t see the form of a table reflected in the eyes, because already we throw away this person, because we are perverted. So, we don’t see it. So, we want to get this true beings, do you understand? Escaping from this perverted, get something true. If you do that, already you don’t see the person who sees this form of a table reflected in the eyes, already. And then, you don’t see this person, and nevertheless you try to get something true; you never get to something true either, because you don’t see, you don’t see who you are, anything. So, if you don’t have a definite clear subject, you cannot get a clear object. Because your subject is very obscure; you don’t know. *[He laughs.]* Very obscure. And then you try to get the object which is true, but you cannot get it, because already object becomes obscure. Do you understand? 

So that’s why Dogen Zenji says, what’s wrong with there form of a table reflected in the eyes? This is nothing but the Buddha’s decoration; so, Buddha’s world. Because if you see deeply to the form of the table reflected in the mirror, then you can touch the core. It’s not separated [from the] truth. 

I mean, always the bottom of *samsara* is kind of a curtain, I say. And, behind the curtain is eternity, I say. Not “bottom”... Anyway, if you research something deeply, then you can touch the dead end. That dead end is not hard iron partition; it’s kind of very soft, very gentle, very meek, you know? If you touch it, *[shoop,]* it disappears. But if you don’t touch it, you can see the partition, curtain. But if you touch it, you don’t see it. So, that is a kind of the situation of the bottom of human samsara, or the dead end of the ultimate research of something. [If you research something] through and through, then you can reach the ultimate state of being. That is [this] curtain. 

So very naturally, the same applies to the form of a table reflected in your eyes; [it] also has this curtain. So what’s wrong with this form of table you can see? So that’s why *flowers in the sky*; table is also a flower in the sky. You can see the tree; this is also a flower in the sky. Snow and severe [weather] is also flowers in the sky. That’s what Dōgen Zenchi tries to say here. 

So that’s “you don’t know the person with cataracts, you don’t see the person with cataracts”; because we always handle the subject and the object dualistically. At that time, you can see the person, you [cannot] see the object clearly. Always they become immediately obscure, very blurry. 

That’s why we have to deal with the subject and the object as a Buddha, from the beginning to end, under all circumstances. But usually we don’t deal with things as a Buddha under all circumstances. Only when you feel good, you can deal with it. When you don’t feel [good], you throw away or break it. And scream, and hate... and you die, commit suicide; something like that. 

Lately, people easily, simply kill the person and commit suicide. I heard in the United States, something happens and people argue on the freeway, talking about the accident, and finally the person killed the partner with a gun. That’s ridiculous. But such a thing happens always. Because they never see the person, they never see the person with eyes with cataracts. In other words, they don’t know how to deal with this person or that person, or the accident or cars and freeways, as a Buddha. They don’t know! Only when they feel good, they can deal with something wonderful, but if they don’t feel good, they kill, they destroy something. That is not appropriate human behavior, because we are already Buddha, so we have to behave as a Buddha. Wherever you may be and under all circumstances, this is our responsibility. If you have a responsibility as a human being, you have to do this! Regardless of whether you understand or not. 

If you try to do otherwise, you never have a chance to build up a peaceful world. *Peaceful world* can be erected or can be built up by you, who builds up, who sees [yourself] clearly, peacefully. At that time you are a peaceful person; *then*, a peaceful world comes up. 

Peaceful world never exists separate from you, and then you can go into the peaceful world. No, you cannot do it. At that time you never have a peaceful world. First, *you* must be a peaceful person, you must be *flowers in the sky*. This is important first. Later Dōgen Zenji talks about this, too. 

So that’s why Dōgen Zenji repeats almost the same thing. They don’t know, they don’t see the person with cataracts, do not meet the person with cataracts, are not the person with cataracts. 

#### 34:49

> One should meet *the person with cataracts*, know *the sky*, and see *the flowers in the sky* too. After seeing the *flowers in the sky*, one should also see *the flowers perish in the sky*. 

Of course. 

> To think that once the *flowers in the sky* cease they should not exist anymore is the view of a small vehicle. If the *flowers in the sky* were not seen, what would they
be? (Those with the view of the small vehicle) only know *flowers in the sky* as something to be abandoned, and do not know the *great matter* after the flowers in the sky –– they do not know the planting, ripening, and shedding of the *flowers in the sky*.
>
> – From *Shōbōgenzō: Zen Essays by Dōgen*, translated by Thomas Cleary.

If the flowers in the sky perish and then this is attaining enlightenment, or nothing is left, we say so. But nothing is left, for instance, the autumn comes, and all the leaves fall. And then all gone. So *all gone* is very clear. Then we feel good, you know? We attain *enlightenment*; nothing to interrupt *my* life. And then this the autumn; so we don’t care about winter, we don’t care spring and summer, et cetera. But after perishing, after all things fall, that is something important you have to take into account. Because still it’s alive. What is the dead leaf? Dead leaf is what? It is nothing but the total manifestation of [all] seasons. Not only the autumn. Through the one dead leaf, you have to see the whole four seasons working together. That’s why after leaving falling leaves, then there is something important for us we have to take into account. You become emptiness, you attain enlightenment, abd then is that the end of practice? No. *After* attaining enlightenment, *after* getting the feeling of peaceful, then that is important. Most people say, “I am peaceful, then I don’t need anything.” And they expect tomorrow will be peaceful. Now I am peaceful through the practice, so I don’t need anything. Tomorrow is peaceful; I don’t need money, I don’t need anything, I don’t need any help, I don’t need the Buddha’s teachings. I am completely peaceful. That is a person who doesn’t see *the person who has eyes with cataracts*. They don’t know how to deal with human bodies, and also they don’t know how to deal with something invisible as a Buddha. You have to deal with the visible and invisible; many things visible and invisible. 

Even if you think something in your head, creating a fiction; so-called, what would say, space being. You can imagine any kind of form of a space being, you know? *[He chuckles.]* So that is why you can create a fiction. That fiction is what? Fiction is fiction, which has nothing to do with us? Yes it [does], because it comes from your head. Where is your head? Your head is the top of the life. So, fiction is also pretty important. Sometimes fiction comes to be true. *[He laughs.]* Don’t you think? 

So, including fiction, including imagination, including delusions, anyway we have to deal with it as Buddha, because they are flowers in the sky. 

So that is after enlightenment, after becoming peaceful, then there is something important you have to take into account. If you say, “I don’t need anything, I don’t need any help,” that is *arrogance*. It’s really arrogant. 

So if you become a peaceful person, then you need *more* careful practice. Bodhisattvas live their life exactly like that. 

So, they...

> ... only know *flowers in the sky* as something to be abandoned, and do not know the *great matter* after the flowers in the sky –– they do not know the planting, ripening, and shedding of the *flowers in the sky*.

Because here it says, they don’t know the planting, ripening, and shedding of the flowers in the sky. I mentioned, even though one thing perishes, still there are many beings around. If you see a human problem, it is kind of a pyramid: the problem you can see now is just the top of the pyramid. Then the problem is gone, but still lots of beings exist after the trouble perishing. So, still important things are there that we have to deal with. That’s why here it says, “they do not know the planting, ripening, and shedding of the flowers.” Still there is a cause and ripening, effect, and constantly shedding of the flowers, always there. 

#### 42:08

> Ordinary scholars today think that where the sun energy abides is *the sky*, that where the sun, moon, planets, and stars hang is *the sky*, and due to that they think that saying *flowers in the sky* means the appearance of forms like floating clouds in this clear air, flying flowers blown hither and thither in the wind rising and falling. 

So, just like imaginative pictures. [...] We believe that is *the sky*, and also that is the *flowers in the sky*. 

> They are far from knowing that the creating and created four gross elements, ...

In the *Sandokai* we say that “water wets, wind moves.” That is creating four gross elements, there. And “creating and created” – do you understand? *Creating* means the subject, means the wind. [Created] gross element means the movement. 

So, 

> ... the creating and created four gross elements, all the phenomena of the material world, as well as fundamental enlightenment, fundamental nature, and so on, are called *flowers in the sky*.

Okay? Here are the four gross elements; that means the whole world. Creating and created four gross elements means the whole world. The whole world and all things in the world. That is why next it says, “all the phenomena of the material world,” including “fundamental enlightenment, fundamental nature, and so on are called *flowers in the sky*.” So all things, visible and invisible are called *flowers in the sky*, through which we can get the robe and seat and room of the Buddha Shakyamuni. 

> Also they do not know that the creative four elements and so on exist due to phenomena; they do not know that the material world remains in its normal state due to phenomena. They only see phenomena as existing due to the material world. 

I mentioned this before. First, there is a world as an object, then we can be born in this world. Okay? Or, we say, here is a peaceful world, then I can get into the peaceful world, then I can be peaceful. That is, we don’t know exactly. If you believe the world is going like that, I think you never get peace and harmony. 

So, that is what he mentions here. Is that okay? 

*[Tape change.]*

... flower blooms. Flower blooms is simultaneously the world arises.

If you become peaceful, you are [that] peaceful world. Because naturally, people around you become peaceful. 

So, very naturally the world comes up. That is what Dōgen Zenji says here. 

So then, “They only see phenomena as existing due to the material world...”

> They only realize that there are *flowers in the sky* due to *cataracted eyes*; they do not realize the principle that *cataract eyes* are caused to exist by *flowers in the sky*. 

Yes. This is cataracted eyes are caused to exist by flowers in the sky. Consciousness – what’s wrong with the consciousness? Consciousness creates perverted view, but perverted view can be caused to exist *by what*? By human delusion? No. By somebody else? No. By *something*, more than you can think. Because that is already there.

So consciousness has already great right to exist, to be in this world. That is called *flower in the sky*. That’s why by flowers in the sky, consciousness is caused to exist. Your stinky body is also caused to exist by what? By Buddha. So that’s why your body is important. Without [a] body, how can you practice, you know? 

So, that is, here it says:

> Know that the *person with cataracts* spoken of by the Buddha is the originally enlightened person, the ineffably enlightened person, the person of the Buddhas, the person of the three worlds, the person beyond Buddha. 

So only the enlightened person, ineffably enlightened person can see these flowers in the sky. In other words, this person can deal with the table as a Buddha; something more than the table. Regardless of whether you understand or not, that person can do it. Okay? Day to day, let’s do it, let’s do it. This is really great cause of creating peaceful world. 

If you forget this, you never create a peaceful world. 

> Do not ignorantly consider *cataracts* to be delusive factors and thus study as if there were something else which is real – that would be a small view. If cataract flowers are delusions, the agent and action wrongly clinging to them as delusions would have to be delusions. If they are all delusions, there can be no logical reasoning.

So, your reasoning and your right understanding becomes also delusions, you know? If your consciousness is delusions, if your consciousness is anyway creating perverted view. So whatever kind of things you can think, all are delusions. So, finally where you are going? Nowhere. So finally you should choose two choices. Live, to survive, or to die. That’s it.

That’s why here says, if they are all delusions, delusions, there can be no logical reasoning.

> If there is no reason established, the fact that cataract flowers are delusions cannot be so.

No way. 

So, delusions, enlightenment – if you believe consciousness is delusions, if you believe even the small things become delusions, I think all things become delusions. So, how can I live? So, that’s why Dōgen Zenji constantly says that the consciousness is also nothing but the flowers in the sky. 

That’s all. Do you have a question? 

#### 51:56

**Question:** I have a question. Imaginary beings, or space beings, or repetitious thoughts, or imagination – is that activity that is second, third moment thought activity, following another thought? Or does that arise spontaneously, with no definite origin? Or what’s understood about it? 

**Katagiri Roshi:** The same things. Even fictions and imaginations are exactly the same as usual thinking, usual thought, usual idea. Same thing.
