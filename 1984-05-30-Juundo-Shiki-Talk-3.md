Although now for some period you are guest or host, later you will be buddha ancestors equally throughout time. Therefore you should not forget the feeling of gratitude. **Katagiri Roshi:** ... But in a sense, you want. 

So according to your desire – I don't think you can set up a sesshin like this. So completely beyond your speculation, *sesshin* is here. That's here, in reality. That reality puts you right in the middle of the zazen; even Katagiri is put right there. There is an opportunity; a wonderful opportunity. 

Only the human mind, the speculation and the intellectual understanding, are always grumbling. 

But we should really appreciate this chance. If you go back to the city, and live at your home, with a friend or alone – can you do this *sesshin*? I don't think [so]. Maybe some of you can do it, but *I* cannot do it. I have been drawn into the Buddha’s world by buddhas – all of you. Not my will, not my intentions. My intention, my will is very weak, very slovenly. I know myself very well. 

That's why when I [have a location], immediately I see myself, exactly. And then come back to the [sign]; that I know myself, how weak I am. So it's really hard to adjust my life to fit that situation.

So anyway, this is complete beyond the speculation: opportunity now and here completely are given to you beyond your [spirit]. 

So [wago], peace and harmony, is to put yourself in that situation, in that opportunity, now and here, fitting yourself exactly to it. Just jump into it. Plant into that opportunities. Not recklessly; with true heart. If you find a real opportunity – this is a real opportunity – you can deal with this opportunity not recklessly or carelessly, but with really sincere heart. 

But usually we don't appreciate it. We are not grateful for this situation. Because if you have a *sesshin*, very naturally you feel pain, and then immediately you are beaten by the pain, so-called circumstances, zazen. Then you don't appreciate, you are not grateful for this situation, because you are beaten by circumstances. [What can you do]? Whatever you feel, you like a *sesshin* or you don't like a *sesshin*, anyway, if you sit together like this, visibly or invisibly, you can finish *sesshin* for seven days. Can you imagine? Can you do a seven day *sesshin* yourself? It's very hard. Probably you can do it, but I cannot do it by myself. But if you practice like this together, you can do it, you can finish seven days. Can you imagine? This is great help. This is a great, real opportunity. Whoever you are, young and old, it doesn't matter. You should appreciate [this]. Exactly others. It seems to me that this kind of situation is really compassionate, generous, and magnanimous situation, by which you can walk hand in hand together in peace in harmony. This is this situation. You cannot do this in the usual life. 

When you get up in the morning, you really get up in the morning, and chant a verse: “I wake up this morning, with all sentient beings, realizing everything without exception, embracing all sentient beings.” *The ten directions* means all sentient beings. You don’t do it in your everyday life. But even for a moment, you can really put yourself right there, right in the middle of all sentient beings, and then you act, so-called getting up in the morning. Not you getting up in the morning in the small territory, so-called “my world.” The moment when you chant this verse, you can act in the universe, and then you can get up. 

That’s why you say, “As we wake up this morning, may all sentient being realize everything without exception.” Embracing all sentient beings. This is a practice of getting up in the morning. It’s very rare, a very rare opportunity. Because we don’t do it every day. But if you are here, sometimes you forget once or twice, but sometimes, even later, you realize, “Oh, I forgot!” So, after washing your face, maybe you can sit down and you can chant. Don’t you think so? That’s wonderful. 

So, therefore, you shouldn’t forget the feeling of gratitude for that which you meet each other on the real opportunity to do and practice what is rare to practice. If you can practice like this, you become a very brave person. *Vajra* person. You know the *vajra*? *Vajra* is very hard; unmovable. Unmovable does not mean stubborn: very stable, majestic. You should be a brave and majestic person. That is [that] you can use this opportunity which is very rare. You can use it. If you cannot be a majestic person and a brave person, you cannot do it. Immediately, you are stumbling, you are beaten by the circumstances. Many circumstances come up: pain, a sense of uselessness, usefulness, sleepiness, many things come up. Then you are really beaten by [circumstances]. If you are beaten by circumstances, immediately you are grumbling about circumstances! The more you are grumbling, the more you are stumbling again, finally you can get up. This is very common.

But if you do this like this, even though you stumble, [still] anyway, visibly or invisibly, people put you right in the middle of the practice of the Buddha way. Like putting yourself in a zigzag type your life in the bamboo stick: naturally you become straight. Do you understand this one? You don’t like a straight [tube] because you always love zigzag type of life. That’s why if you are put in the bamboo, you don’t like it. But that is pretty good, because you can know how zig-zag type you are, how much you love that. 

So someday, you have to put yourself like this, in the bamboo stick.

> This is called the body and mind of buddha-dharma; you will certainly become a buddha ancestor.
> 
> – From “Regulations for the Auxiliary Cloud Hall (Jūundō-shiki)” in *Moon in a Dewdrop*, edited by Kazuaki Tanahashi.

“This is called the body and mind of buddha-dharma”: this means opportunity you can meet each other on rare opportunity and practice what is rare, true practice. This modified this sentence. So this opportunity which you can meet is very rare, with each other, practice which is rare to practice is what is called buddha ancestor. The body and mind of the buddha ancestor. We don’t understand this one, but according to what I said for two days so far on the way seeking mind: the way seeking mind is the mainspring of burning your energy, vitality. That is the way seeking mind. Drive your engine, drive your ship. Constantly, you cannot stop it. You have to move; you have to proceed. And also, in the process of proceeding, you have to continually learn all sentient beings. It’s impossible, but you have to learn all sentient beings, which is impossible for you to learn all. That’s why you have to learn, constantly. You have to make a determination to learn: how? Continually, you have to learn. But it’s pretty hard. That’s why with vow, repentence, reflection, and prayer, we can make a determination of learning continually; the whole situation, all sentient beings, which is impossible for us to learn all, in this life. 

And then that, your vitality, original vitality, and continuation of learning with vow, with samadhi wisdom – you are exactly put right in the middle of the right course. That is the ship in reality, the existence of the ship in reality, in the huge ocean. Completely beyond; that is reality, not a matter of discussion. So-called *unity*; I say unity of input and output. If I say “unity of input and output,” it is already something conceptualized. But reality, the ship is completely put right in the middle of right course, is completely beyond the speculation. [It’s] reality; which is silent, quiet, but it’s dynamically moving with the ocean. 

And then after that, we can say [something], we can explain what is the total panoramic picture of the ship’s movement right in the middle of the ocean. How? It’s what? We say, unity of input and output. That is [an idea]; but actually you don’t know exactly the total movement of the ship. Even the ship doesn’t know. Because it’s reality, fact. 

That’s why you can experience, before you explain: you are real, really there, right there. Then you can experience; completely beyond like or dislike, you can experience. And then after that, you can explain. 

So the system of science and the system of the intellectual world is really coming after your direct experience. But, we don’t do it [that way]. First, you try to understand the intellectual world, how the world is structured, and then you try to put your life into it. That’s opposite. That would be really reversed. 

Well, it is important for us; teaching and thought are important. But you should pay attention to *real reality* you are present in from moment to moment, *before* you think it.

So, this is *body and mind are buddha-dharma*. Exactly your body and mind is right in the middle of unity of whole universe, which is working together in peace and harmony. 

That is what? Just do it, [with sincerity]. Or, if I say so, please, don’t forget the feeling of gratitude for this opportunity. *Live there*, with sincerity. And then you can learn gradually, “Aha, this is our life, human life.” But if you grow this reality, real opportunity, and try to understand, you never have a chance to understand the panoramic picture of human life.

But first of all, you have to fit yourself into this real opportunity, [what] we call *en*. *En* means conditions, or interdependent conditions. 

That is called peace; exactly peace. Fitting into, planting into that rare opportunity, with sincere heart. Not blindly. Not for the sake of satisfying your desires. Completely beyond satisfaction of your desire or not, anyway, you have to fit, you have to plant [yourself] into that rare opportunity with sincere heart. That is called peace, harmony. 

After that, we can understand the structure, the teaching, [what we call] *interdependent co-origination*. But that is ideas, teaching. You don’t even understand that teaching, because it’s very complicated. But practically speaking, it’s pretty easy: every day, open to that situation. So all you have to do is just plunge into that [reality]. 

Usually people very often ask me the difference [between] Rinzai and Sōtō. Rinzai uses koans; Sōtō doesn’t use koans. So people ask me: if you don’t use a koan, what [is it useful for]? In other words, for what do you zazen? If you don’t emphasize attaining enlightenment using a koan, et cetera, there is no particular sweet candy. So very naturally you can [ask]: for what do you do zazen? [If there is] no particular aim, so-called enlightenment, or delusions, or confusion, nothing, and no use of koans, et cetera... if so, for what do you do zazen? 

I can tell you: you should do zazen just for doing zazen. 

You should become zazen, what it is. When you have to do zazen, that’s all you have to do.

And then you say, “It’s ridiculous!” That’s all right! It’s ridiculous.

If you believe it is ridiculous... well, let’s say, you might be an outcast from human society, because you do something ridiculous. And then very naturally, people [say], “Hey, you are a crazy guy.” And then maybe you starve to death. 

[So] why don’t you starve to death? Can you starve to death? Can you have this great determination to starve to death because you do ridiculous zazen? 

You don’t believe this, but if you do this, [...] you are really *brave*; an [eminent] person, who influences the whole universe, [and] human culture; you can really help the development of human civilization. 

Even though you don’t believe me now, sooner or later, everything becomes like this; everything follows in this teaching. Not only Buddhism, not only human life; *any* kind of science will follow this teaching, sooner or later. In the 21st century: sure! Already science is starting to follow this way. And then the science will show you: “Yes, it is a fact. If you don’t follow this teaching, you cannot use my science.” And then you are really confused. “Hey, let’s use that.” 

But if I say so now, you don’t believe me. That’s all right. *[He chuckles.]* [...] Anyway, even [so] ... spiritual life is just like this. 

So, if you do zazen *for the sake of* – if you say *for the sake of* – it’s pretty easy to do zazen, because there is some purpose. 

Well, there is some purpose in Zen Buddhism. For what do you do zazen? To become Buddha. And then if you say, “I do zazen for the sake of becoming Buddha,” immediately your lifestyle becomes exactly the same as usual life, so-called *dualistic*. If you live in the dualistic, very naturally the bottom of your life becomes *shaky*, just like an earthquake. Because it’s not stable. So that’s why idealistically, of course, you should be Buddha. But becoming Buddha is not *in the future*; [...] to do zazen with your body and mind *is* simultaneously to become Buddha. So no dualism. If I explain it, it becomes dualistic; but practically, it is just one. 

You never know. Just like a ship. Ship is moving right in the middle of ocean – quietly, but it’s moving. 

And then how does that ship move? We say unity of input and output; that is the explanation. But the explanation of unity of input and output is nothing but an explanation; it doesn’t work. So you have to understand the unity of the input and output, and then you should *live*, beyond unity of input and output. You should know *what is* real reality beyond the idea of unity of input and output. There is a ship *in movement*, reality from moment to moment. Which is quiet; but it goes, even if you don’t know. 

So that is *the body and mind of buddha-dharma*. You will certainly become a Buddha ancestor. Anyway, buddha and ancestors practice like this.

> You have left your home and birthplace. You depend on clouds and you depend on water. 
>
> – From “Regulations for the Auxiliary Cloud Hall (Jūundō-shiki)” in *Moon in a Dewdrop*, edited by Kazuaki Tanahashi.

Clouds and water [means] human life is helped by people and grasses, birds, skies, [nations], weather. Rainy days, snowy days. You cannot live alone. You cannot take care of yourself by yourself. That’s why you depend on clouds and water. It means the whole universe, every day. Rare opportunity, now and here. Next moment. The whole situation: you really depend upon the whole situation, the panoramic picture of the universe. Which is just like a cloud: as it really is. Cloud is moving constantly, hanging in the sky – but nothing to hang on to. It’s moving, but it’s there, in the sky. Just like water. 

There is nothing to hang on for you, but you exist. Who supports your life? The universe supports your life? Oh of course, but it’s still idealistical. You should pay attention to your life in reality, which is really supported by the universe, *before* you think of it. *How?* Anyway, proceed, and collect information, with vow, and prayer, et cetera. That is what? Just be quiet; just *do it*. 

Just like a ship, moving. That is zazen: a typical type of practice for us; very simple and *condensed*. Because the ship in reality is very correct. It is fact! Zazen is exactly like this: *sit down there*. How does this zazen-in-reality work? And then that is 18,000 Buddhist scriptures. That is a huge mansion. And then we hang on to eighteen thousand scriptures: “Yes, I believe this.” And then we completely forget the life in reality which is moving, [our] rare opportunity, from day to day, from moment to moment.

> The helpfulness of this assembly in support of you and your practice surpasses that which was given by your father and mother. Your father and mother are temporarily close to you in birth and death, but this assembly of students is your companion in the way of enlightenment for all time.
>
> – From “Regulations for the Auxiliary Cloud Hall (Jūundō-shiki)” in *Moon in a Dewdrop*, edited by Kazuaki Tanahashi; modified by Katagiri Roshi.

Maybe you have lots of questions about this, but I don’t mean you should disrespect your parents, mother and father, because your mother and father let you be born in this world, and raised your life. But the people in the sangha are something more than father and mother, because they make you *mature*. Mature, and peaceful. Visibly or invisibly, the people in the sangha put you [to] work exactly in peace and harmony. Just like a ship moving, every day. 

The unity of input and output is this community, or *sangha*, or Buddha’s world. Anyway people in the sangha, visibly or invisibly, put you to live in that universal life, so friends in the sangha make your life mature and deep. That’s why your friends in the sangha are very important. 

But your friends in the sangha and parents are no different, because they live in the Buddha’s world. But, there *is* a differentiation there. In the Buddha’s world, flower is flower, bird is bird. So you have to deal with the bird as bird as it is: who lives in the Buddha’s world. So you can really take care of it with sincere heart. But bird is different from grasses, human beings, sky. When you see the grasses, you should know what the grass is, and deal with the grass as it is. But it is not creating discrimination between them. That’s why, basically, you have to respect. Respect is to deal with your object [and] yourself *with* respect. Not *thinking* respect, a sense of respect; no. You should manifest, and *deal with* something *with* respect. That is a practice for us. 

That’s why Dōgen Zenji says here, “You father and mother are important, but temporarily close to you in birth and death.” They are very close to you. But parents are parents. Not matter how old you become – 50, 60, 70 – right in front of your parents, you’re still a little boy, little girl. Because parents really have a strong attachment, emotionally, intellectually to you. 

So it’s very difficult to know the panoramic picture of the human world in the universal perspective. But you can know human feelings, emotions: you can know that small world. How strong an attachement is there: yes, you can know. That’s why you feel good, you know? You feel good. But that attachment really puts you right in the middle of a small world. But we have to collect *huge* information, from huge worlds: all sentient beings. Not only information from an emotional world or sentimental world. Intellectual or sentimental: *beyond* that, visibly and invisibly, we have to collect the information from *many* beings.

That’s why Dōgen Zenji says, “But this assembly is your company in the way of enlightenment for all time.” Your friends in the sangha, visibly or invisibly, consciously or unconsciously, guide you to enlightenment, universal life. So that’s why we have to live in peace and harmony: because all are great friends for us. 

So in the monastery, the assembly is called *shōjō daikaishu* (清浄大海衆). *Shōjō* (清浄 or 清淨) means “pure and clean.” *Daikaishū* (大海衆) means the “great ocean assembly.” So, “Assembly of Great Ocean which is Pure and Clean”: that is the name of the assembly in the sangha. So when you join in the assembly in the sangha, you become a person who lives in the great ocean which is pure and clean, visibly or invisibly. That’s why we have to live in peace and harmony together, and that is simultaneously to promote [further] Buddha’s Way. For this, we have to practice hard, even if you want to be lazy or slovenly. Not for the sake of *your* life; you should think carefully of the next generations, for others. 

Anyway, we have to practice seriously, hard, now. And then, you can promote. Otherwise, in the later generations, very naturally, Buddhism, whatever, human culture, human life, is becoming lazy. 

The more civilization is developed, human beings are very lazy, because you can push a button, and then the result comes up very naturally. Even though you don’t have *any* idea what’s going on *between*, you don’t care, because it’s very quick, and it makes you makes your life pretty nice. It’s lots of fun. So push a button, and get the result. But, I don’t think that is the final goal for us.
