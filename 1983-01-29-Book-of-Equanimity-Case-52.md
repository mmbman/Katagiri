**Katagiri Roshi:** In the *Book of Equanimity*, there is an interesting question and answer between the Zen teacher and his student. Case 52: 

> Caoshan (Sozan Zen Master) asked Elder De, “‘The buddha’s true reality body is like space: it manifests form in response to beings, like the moon in the water.’ How do you explain the principle of response?”
> 
> De said, “Like an ass looking in a well.”

#### 2:00

> Caoshan asked Elder Monk De, “‘The buddha’s true reality body...’” 

That may be understood as the ultimate state of mind-heart. The very ultimate state of mind and heart is manifesting in the simplest way. It’s very simple. That is called, we say, *oneness*. Very simple, but in the external world, including the material world and also the state of consciousness you can see, they are very complicated, *very* complicated. But the real ultimate state of mind-heart is very simple. So that is here called “the buddha’s true reality dharma.” Originally it says “true dharma body.” It means the real state of the truth, what the real truth is.

So here it says “the buddha’s true reality body is like space.” *Space* here is represented as *akasha* in Sanskrit. *Akasha* means space or sky. 

This is a particular term in Buddhism. In the *Prajñāpāramitā* Zen scriptures, this term can be found very often. *Akasha* is used to represent space and sky, which means *boundlessness*, *infinity*, or *countless*. That is *space*. 

So in *Abhidharmakosha* – *Abhidharmakosha* is the oldest Buddhist scripture, explaining the human world, human life in terms of the faculty of consciousness – it says *akasha* has a specific quality of *no obstruction*, in which it has its motion. So *akasha* – space or sky – is characterized by *no obstruction*, *no hindrance*, because it’s boundless, it’s infinite, it’s countless. 

And then in this boundlessness, in this infinity, *forms* means the form of a human body, form of a flower, form of a table, can have a chance to move, to function. Is that clear? So only within the *akasha* – space and sky, which means boundless infinity – the form of all sentient beings can make their life alive. 

So where are *you* now? Are you in your own territory, [as] you believe? Mm-mm. (No.) That is the small scale, a small understanding, very shallow understanding. So you are alive now; where are you? Why can you be alive? Because you are in *akasha*. This is a unique chance [or] opportunity for all sentient beings to make their lives alive, or to make their lives manifest themselves, in everyday life. 

So that’s why it says *akasha* has a specific quality of *no obstruction*, *no hindrance*. This infinity, boundlessness, is characterized by *no hindrance*. That means constantly working and functioning, supporting, helping all sentient beings to create their own life. 

It makes sense, really. That is in which it has its motions; you can see the real motion of human life, real aliveness of human life, where? Not in *your* territory. If you see your function or motion in your territory, it *freezes* immediately. So it doesn’t work. Only in the realm of infinity, boundlessness, can you see real function, total working of your life, full aliveness of your life. 

#### 10:24

So let’s read the first sentence: 

> “‘The buddha’s true reality body is like space: ...’”

What is the truth, *real* truth? What is the real state, ultimate state of mind and heart? It is just like space or sky. It is just like motion, with no hindrance. This is your ultimate state of mind-heart, called *truth*, ultimate truth. 

So, Caoshan asked Elder Monk De, “‘The buddha’s true reality body is like space: ...’” 

> “‘...it manifests form in response to beings, ...’”

In other words, how? How does this ultimate state of mind-heart respond to its own being? In other words, truth, the real, ultimate state of your mind is not something [like a] ghost. It's not something “foggy.” It's working in your life; if it is working in your life, it must manifest itself as a being. 

Because you cannot exist without awareness. If you exist in this world, you have to awaken *you*, awaken [to] yourself, who you are. And then you really feel a sense of *subjectivity*, which is working in your life. This is the bedrock of human function, constantly. If something is working, very naturally it has to know, well, what it is. That is called *awareness*. 

So for instance, if Katagiri – this guy – really works in his life, he has to awaken to his life. Without awareness, I cannot make my life fully alive. If I don't awaken, my life is working, but very naturally, sooner or later, it becomes messy. 

So if my life is constantly fully functioning, what [does it] mean? Well, I have to know. So that is the characteristic of *motion*, *aliveness*. If you say *I am alive*, *I am present*, at that time, without awareness you cannot say *I am present*. No, you cannot say so. If you say *I am present*, that presence must be backed by deep awakening. At that time it is called a little presence, your presence. 

The same applies to the ultimate state of mind and heart, which is just functioning, fully, with no obstructions. So that is called *truth*. So truth itself is characterized by knowing what it is. So very naturally, how to know? In order to know it, it has to respond to its own being – taking form. And then it appears; simultaneously it appears. 

So when it appears, that’s why it says “it manifests form”: simultaneously “it manifests form in response to beings.” 

#### 16:25

> “‘...like the moon in the water.’ ...”

What do we mean by *response*? How response? We want to know how truth manifests on the surface of the water, so-called *being*, taking form. How can truth manifest? We want to know. We always separate truth and the phenomenal world, form. Always the form of the human world; but truth doesn't have a particular form, because it's functioning, so-called energy. So we don't know, we don't know. But it manifests, because it is functioning; if it functions, very naturally it appears. How does it appear on the human world? That's pretty hard to know. But that's what intellect always researches: how? We want to know, we want to stop up this gap between the phenomenal world and truth. 

You do always do this stopping up, always – but you never stop [it] up with your knowledge, because simultaneously it's working. *Simultaneously* is not good enough, it's not the appropriate term. I don't know how, so that's why we say *immediately* or *simultaneously*. We say so, but it's not an *adverb*, it's a *verb*. Anyway it's working. *Simultaneity*... but it's not a noun. *[He laughs.]* I don't know any [functioning] term, so I don't know how you can express it. So *simultaneously*, anyway, it works like this. 

So that's why it says here, “like the moon in the water.” The moon is [in] the sky, and the water is on the earth. That is the world of human speculation: so then the moon is over there, and the pond is here. How can the truth manifest form in response [to] being? How can the moon manifest itself in response [to] its own being? So simultaneously, shadow, or reflections of the moon. But this is really an intellectual understanding. So very naturally first of all we set all sentient beings in a dualistic way: moon; water. And then how can they both work together, putting them together? So, the moon reflects in the water. We can say so. But it's still dualistic. Do you understand? It's still dualistic, because without setting up the moon and the water in a dualistic way first, you don't see the function, [...] the aliveness, as one. So very naturally we take examples: the moon, and water. But an example is [just] an example; so very naturally, it is still dualistic. 

It's pretty hard to say. Alright? But anyway, he says truth is just like space; just motion without hindrance. If so, how does it manifest itself in response to its own being? So it's just like the relationship between the moon [and] the water.

So that's why it says here, "How do you explain the principle of response?"

So I should maybe stay for ten more years. Maybe I should stay twenty *more* years. And then maybe I could get, I could get freedom – from you, *[laughter,]* from zazen, from Zen Center. “Bye bye!” *[He laughs.]*

But this is Zen practice, okay? It’s pretty nice. You can really [be] relieved. 

Look at your life, always expecting responsiveness. That means you have to really challenge something always: “I challenging *anything*. I must be strong. Get money.” You know? Get the strength, always, don’t you think so? 

Just like a spitz, you know? Barking at dogs. Always barking. Do you understand? The spitz is cute. *[Laughter.]* But barking is not a symbol of strength, you know? *[Laughter.]* Barking is really a sign of weakness; that’s why you have to bark always. 

Only when you are tied up with responsiveness, you can be quiet, because, well, responsiveness is your master, you know? “Hey, here, lots of fun there.” So you can be quiet. But while you can have a master, you are always barking. That means a challenge; always doing. But if you keep away from master, so-called responsiveness, you don’t know what to do. Then, the spitz, anyway, his tail goes down, and then running away, with quietness. Do you understand? 

That’s your life – including me. That’s interesting, isn’t it? 

So – don’t bark, anyway. Even though you *want* to bark – anyway, don’t bark. Don’t bark *back*, anyway. Because already the world barks at you, every time, wherever you may go. If you bark back, it’s trouble for you. So be quiet. Anyway, just keep your life. You know, practicing just like this, with non-responsiveness. That’s pretty good. And then, you can see real strength. Real strength. 

If you become really strong, you become quiet, anyway. Everyone. Everyone becomes just like that. If you are not strong, you are always barking. 

So, this is *shikantaza*. It’s a really simple practice: just sit down, without barking [about] anything. That’s pretty hard. That’s why, anyway, you have to do it! Alright? 

Do you have questions? 

*[Pause.]*

It’s alright barking at *me* now. *[Laughter.]*

[...] You should be a little bit careful; you should find when, how to bark. Okay? So *now* you go; I give you permission to bark at me. *[He laughs.]*
