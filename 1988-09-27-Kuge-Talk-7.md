#### 0:00

**Katagiri Roshi:** According to Bodhidharma’s statement, I think it is very precious for us to be here and now, or to exist day to day, or to be born in this world. Because beyond your control, the whole universe in many ways supports us and makes our life mature – offering lots of “fertilizers” [for] human life. So very naturally, to be born in this world is very precious. That’s why Dōgen Zenji and also ancestors and buddhas say [how] rare it is to be born as a human being in this world. Not only human beings; to be born as a tree, to be born as a pebble, to be born as a human being, all are precious for us. So, in many ways we have to express our great appreciation to this reality. 

Some people say, if the universe or universal energy supports us and make our life mature, it’s not necessary to practice zazen, or it’s not necessary to have a particular religious practice, et cetera – so why don’t you enjoy your life without doing *anything*? But this is a misunderstanding of the grandiose energy of the universe [which is] offered to us constantly. So I think we should express appreciation to it; to express appreciation is to *do* something for it, or to *manifest* that grandiose nature of energy with our body and mind, in our life. Within this body we should manifest this grandiose, great energy of the whole universe. This is to express appreciation to the universe. 

(*Transcriber’s Note:* The word *grandiose* has a connotation of pretentiousness which was probably not intended; a better word might be *magnificent*. However the Thomas Cleary translation of *Kuge* uses the word *grandiose*, so I have left it as is.)

But usually we don’t believe in that way, because the universe is working beyond our control, beyond our thinking, beyond our perceptions, so why don’t we enjoy our life without doing anything, without suffering, et cetera? But this is a big misunderstanding. 

So very naturally, Dōgen Zenji says here, I think we should practice cause and effect – [that is,] cultivate cause and experience the effect. 

Yesterday I said that cause and effect are not the usual sense of cause and effect we think, but cause and effect in the realm of *common experience* – that means the universe, *cosmos*; in the realm of the cosmos. The commentary says *the realm of the cosmos* means nothing to be hidden throughout the whole universe; this is the realm of the cosmos. 

So you have to learn the cause and effect in the realm of the cosmos, where there is nothing to be hidden throughout the universe. How do we practice this? Yesterday I mentioned, cultivating and experiencing this cause and effect in the realm of the cosmos is to constantly return to the silence of *gassho*, having a meal, walking on the street, talking with people, and giving a massage to somebody, and offering incense, and whatever we do; we have to constantly return to the silence of all activities. And that time you can learn, you can cultivate. This is called *to cultivate cause and experience effect in the realm of the cosmos*: very universal cause and effect. 

[...] We cannot stop doing this. Constantly we have to do this, day to day. There is no limitation, there is no particular stage you have reached. Because everything is moving and acting at super-speed, so there is nothing to stop [at], nothing to stay for certain place or a certain time. So this kind of practice should be continuing *forever*, day to day. 

Dōgen Zenji comments on Bodhidharma’s statement like this; that’s why [it says], 

> Cultivating this cause and effect in the realm of common experience, one experiences cause and effect in the realm of common experience. 
>
> – From *Shōbōgenzō: Zen Essays by Dōgen*, translated by Thomas Cleary.

So from this point, if you continue to do this practice, who are you? Who encourages you to maintain this kind of practice? *You*? Yes, *you*! But *you* are not *you*, because you are others; you are tree. Because you and others, all sentient beings, live in the same and one level, so-called *cause*: Buddha’s world. If we use a technical term, we use *Buddha*, *buddha-nature*, or *dharmakaya*; we say so. But anyway, this is *the same and one ground*. 

If you return to the very incipient state of the moment, everything becomes exactly one – interconnectedly, interpenetrately, without any hindrances there. It’s very strange, but it is really true! In terms of human speculation, it’s impossible to think that, but the world of the very incipient moment is really working like this. Everything is working like this. 

That is called *silence*; absolute silence. That is called sometimes *aloneness*. Because when you stand up there, you alone are there – no one around. But you are really working and supported by all living beings. If you have the eyes to open, to see the depths of your existence, you are alone but you are not alone – you are supported by many beings. At that time you can be *exactly* alone, with stability, with peace, and bliss, and repose. Exactly. 

So when you die... you can die. When you live, you can live. Regardless of whether people are around you or people are not around you, anyway we can live, we can die. 

The words are easy to say, but actually there is no guarantee whether you can do this or not. At the moment of your death, there is no guarantee! So day-to-day, we have to show your guarantee; at the moment, from moment to moment, you have to show the guarantee. This is appreciation: to express appreciation to the universe, appreciation to your existence. That is our practice. Day to day, we do this [like this]. 

So from this point, “of its own accord” is *you*, but you are not you, you are the *all living beings*. 

So because everything is interconnected, interpenetrated, when you realize your existence, you are exactly, perfectly, absolutely alone. Beyond being alone or not being alone, beyond your perceptions, you are *absolutely* alone. *Then*, you can be free from aloneness. At that time, that is called *repose and bliss*. Just *shikantaza*: sitting zazen, exactly. 

[A monk asked] Hyakujō, Pai Chang (Baizhang), “What is the wondrous subtle event in this world?” [Pai Chang] said, “Sitting zazen with wholeheartedness at the peak of the great mountain.” (*Transcriber’s Note:* This story is in *Blue Cliff Record* Case 26.) That is exactly same meaning. You just *sit*; you just *be*; whatever. This is our practice, day to day. This is constantly, forever, everlastingly, you have to carry it on, day to day. Whoever you are – beyond wise or not wise, beyond races, beyond different cultures, anyway human beings should carry this day to day. This is called *spiritual life*. 

Spiritual life is not to get a fantastic spiritual event, or a mystical event or experience. That is not spiritual life. Spiritual life is whether you can be absolutely alone, right now, right here, beyond the perception of aloneness or not aloneness. *Then* this is really spiritual life, called *religion*; Buddha’s teaching, which means the teaching of the ultimate nature of existence. 

So,

> *Own* is the self, the self is definitely *you* – it means the four gross elements and five clusters. 

This is the Buddha’s teaching we talked about. If you try to explain why I am you, you are me, you are all other sentient beings – why? – then the Buddha says it like this: we are structured by the four gross elements and five clusters, et cetera.

> Because of being able to employ the *true human with no position*, it is not “I,” it is not *who*; ...

The *true human with no position*, this is Rinzai Zen Master’s statement to the assembly. 

So I think because the *I* is not always *I*, the *I* is *you* and *other beings* – if so, the *I* doesn’t have a particular frame, or concept, or any label. No labels, no concepts, no stage, no positions. At that time it is called *the self who exists at the very incipient moment*, called *silence*. Then, the self becomes the true human with no position. 

At that time, who are you? *Who* is doing zazen? [There is] no *you*; so it is not you. 

So the self is very deep, nothing to say; but if you start to talk about it, you can say a lot! But if you say a lot, finally you can reach to a certain stage where [there is] nothing to say. So the self is very strange... not strange, this is a true picture of existence. It’s very high, it’s very deep. So if you talk about *you*, you can talk about a lot, forever. Even [if you] pick up a piece of vegetable, you can talk about it a long time, endlessly. But sooner or later you will reach a certain stage of no words, and then all you can do is just to pick it up and *see* it, and *feel* the life of a piece of vegetable. That’s it. Very strange! But very wondrous. Very deep, and very high. 

So we have to cultivate this. *Cultivate* means we have to practice this, called *cause*. This is a cause. *Cause* is Buddha; cause is great being, great existence, as a being in the very incipient moment, *always*. This is our practice. 

Then,

> ... it is not “I,” it is not *who*; ...

Nothing to say – *you* or *I* or *the other* or *who*, nothing to say. Because it is the true human with no position. No color, no taste, no flavors – nothing. It’s just like a “Casper.” But a Casper still has a frame or form. *[Here he is laughing a little. Someone off in the audience laughs.]* If you don’t have any frame and form, you are completely *gone*; it doesn’t make sense for us. That’s why the comic makes a frame for Casper. 

So this is a human being! Each of us is a Casper: very cute, and great activity from this tiny cute being, you know? Helping all sentient beings. *[There is some laughter in the background.]*

So,

> ... therefore being *not compulsory* is called *on its own*. 

So nothing compulsory; nothing to compel you to do this or that. 

> *Ripening of its own accord* is the time of the flower’s opening and forming fruit, it is the time of *communicating the Teaching to save deluded sentient beings*.

So *ripening of its own accord* means to be born in this world is already being *ripened*, of itself. So birth, your presence is exactly ripening of its own. One flower blooms: this is your presence. 

That’s why your presence is important, [for example] for a person who is about to die. [Or whoever it is], your presence is important. Because your presence is a great being, great state of existence beyond your speculation, beyond anything and nothing, beyond compulsion... nothing to compel you. So beyond your perception, beyond morality, beyond ethics, beyond religion, your presence is exactly your presence. That’s it!

That is called *time of the flower’s opening*. That’s why I sometimes say that all you can practice is to let the flower of your presence bloom. Day to day, from moment to moment: bloom!

So the flower’s opening is your presence from moment to moment; this is flower’s opening. And this is simultaneously forming the fruit, because it affects others, simultaneously. And also it affects *you*, because that presence right now, right here, beyond your speculation, makes your life mature, simultaneously. And also supporting you, supporting others. And others support you, you support you. That is called *ripening of its own accord*. 

So from this point, in this state of existence, we can communicate the teaching, we can give, we can offer the dharma to people, to sentient beings. *And* also that is [that] to teach the dharma, to teach the sublimity of human being, how precious it is, [that] *is* to save others, to make people relieved, completely relieved, offering relaxation and assurance of existence – repose and bliss, exactly. That’s why your presence is perfect, and also absolute, and also important; precious for us. 

From this point, your life is never your life. Your life is *something*. But can you deal with your life beyond perception of *yours*? 

We understand that intellectually, but day to day we don’t do it. For instance, particularly when you face difficulties, I think it’s pretty difficult to see your life which is *not* yours. Because when you see difficulties in your life, you are always looking at your difficulties, and you make the difficulties turn into suffering, because you are always handling your life which is just yours. That’s why you always dwell in your own territory, and watch your difficulties, and try to do something *for* it, by *you*. 

So finally, there are only two choices: live, die. Or be here, or escape. Those two choices happen. Because difficulties turn into the suffering, and suffering creates a feeling of like or dislike, lots of emotion coming up – that makes [it] difficult [for you] to *be*. So very naturally that makes you avoid, or remove from one to another. 

How can I say it... Lately, not only in the United States but in Japan too, young people commit suicide. Because society is very complicated, and creating pollution, and creating lots of problems, in the human society, in Japan, in the United States. So young people [find it] very difficult to find hope for their future life. So very naturally they are disappointed to live. Because they unconsciously deal with their life by their own life, believing “this is my life.” There is no space, no time to let it go, open. Always be a “hoarder,” or “hang around” always: “This is my life, this is my life.” 

So that’s why particularly if you see difficulties, you are completely confined into a small shell, so-called *difficulties*. And [then you are] just like an octopus: the difficulties [are like] a kind of a pot that the octopus is in. If the pot is moving, then the octopus always becomes rigid and sticks to the inside of the pot; they don’t know how to escape from it. Actually it is very sure they will be caught by a human being, but they don’t know how to escape. The same thing happens in the human world, human life; because we always believe this is *my* life; never open. 

So finally, there is no way to go. Only two ways to go: that is to live or to die. That’s it; no in-between. That is always happening. That’s why young people say, “I want to die.” Or [even if] you don’t [want to] die, sometimes we feel, “Oh, I should die, because human life is very difficult. I don’t want to live in this world like this.” So that means already unconsciously you are dealing with your life as *yours*. There is no space, no time to *open*; no time, no space to share your life with all other beings. Always we believe, “this is mine.” 

Just like a scientist or a [biologist]: always studying science and physics, and then the sciences and molecular biologists believe [...] life is just like a machine. Because life has been researched very deeply; like DNA, you know? Finally we can control life, because the origin of life is already analyzed; they can know how the origin of life is structured. So naturally they believe that life is no longer *life*, but a machine. A piece of machine; that’s it. They seriously believe it!

But this is not [just] scientific thought, this is also Buddhist thought too. Because we believe Buddhism is *best*. Do you understand? [Or] Christianity is best. The more you study or practice Christianity deeply, they believe really this is a great religion in the universe. *[He laughs.]* Yes it is, but that is really arrogance. Because there is no space, no time to open, sharing it with all sentient beings. 

If you continue to do this, I think there is no freedom. The freedom open for them is only two choices: to die or to be alive; that’s it. That’s difficulties. That is called human problems. 

So I think of course in human society we don’t believe [this,] *anyone*. [...] “Katagiri’s life is not Katagiri’s life” – *no one* believes this one. Because society already makes certain rules, regulations, constitutions, and concepts, ideas: this is Katagiri’s life, this is [someone else’s] life, this is the table’s life, you know? Already we set up all things, and then we live there. So we never get out of this and see our life once again from a different angle. We don’t do it. 

I think the regulations and rules we have set up are fine; we should live there. But I think we should get out of it – not get out of it – we should see our life in the human society from a different angle once again. This is our practice, okay? This is our practice. 

So *just sitting* is very difficult for us. And *your sitting is Buddha*; not *Buddha will come to you in the future*. Or *even though you are just sitting forever, there is some guarantee that you will be Buddha*. No. I don’t think this is the practice. Okay? 

So very naturally, someone is always practicing zazen seriously with the right posture, someone is sitting just in a relaxed posture, but they are continuing to sit. Which is better? We don’t know which is better. Both are better, both are nice, because both believe very strongly [that] we *will* be Buddha by practicing zazen. That is *individual belief*! Do you understand this? At that time you will never be Buddha. *[He laughs.]* Because this is the ego; nothing but arrogance. 

So the different view of human life is [that] you become Buddha *right now* if you do it. If you return to silence of zazen, which is called *the very incipient moment*, then you can be Buddha, naturally. That is called *one flower opening, and fruits come up*. We don’t believe this, but that is most the important practice which you have never done before. But this is universal. Whatever age comes – 20th century, 21st century – this is important for us. That’s why we have to do it. Very simple, but in the simplicity there is lots of meaning there. 

I’m not talking about [just] all of *your* lives, but including *my* life. I always feel about my life: terrible life. *[He laughs.]* I’m not always happy, you know? Everyone believes if you become a Zen teacher, so-called “Zen master”... I don’t like this term, *Zen master*. But [people believe] if you become a Zen master, you don’t suffer anymore. That is completely opposite, okay? *[Laughter.]* Believe me. *[He laughs.]* 

I am the same [kind of] human being as your life. But a point is, how do I stand up right in the midst of the suffering which everyone has? You have suffering, I have suffering; but there is a little different *attitude*, just a *little* different *state* of human life. That is your attitude, that is your living; how to live, day to day. 

Generally, I have lots of suffering: I don’t like this, I like that, always. So I want to escape from Zen Center, and I want to live in Japan.... but when I went to Japan, I felt also alone, because nobody [dealt with] me [as] I was. Because I didn’t live in Japan for a long time, everything has changed so much, so the Japanese situation is completely out of me. *[He laughs.]* So even though I talk [in gear], my talking [isn’t in gear] exactly. So naturally I was kicked out. Naturally; not by [anything] compulsory. *[He laughs.]* And then I don’t like Japan, so I would like to go back to United States, because the United States is open! So I come back here... and then another suffering comes up! *[He laughs.]* *[Laughter.]* 

So I thought, which way to go? Should I live, or should I die? So finally I thought “I should die, I don’t want to live in this life,” like that; going here and going there, always. And thinking, this is I like or I don’t like, always going here and there – it’s very difficult. So finally I thought I should die, you know? At any time, it is alright to die. But while I am saying I am ready to die, I cannot die, *[he laughs,]* because still I am caught by the concept of death, which brings up simultaneously the concept of life into me. *[He laughs.]* So while I am screaming “I want to die,” on the other hand the different I who is screaming “I want to live” is there. So finally I couldn’t die!

So nowhere to go, but only two choices: to die or to live. That’s why we are very crazy; we  naturally become crazy, because only two choices, nowhere to go beyond this, if you always believe “this is my life, and my life should be controlled by my powers.” Even if you don’t believe [this], unconsciously we do it. From now, watch your life carefully: we always do it. Particularly when you think “I want to die,” at that time you believe immediately, simultaneously, you can control your life by your powers. Because you believe very strongly your life is yours. But it is not true. It is not completely true. 

That’s why you have to *share* your life with all sentient beings. If you cry, everyone cries. Do you believe this one? [If you cry,] everything cries. If you smile, everything smiles. If you scream, everything screams. Can you accept this scream [where] everything screams, through your body and mind? It’s very hard; but you should do it. 

If you take responsibility for this true reality – when you cry, everything cries – then right in the middle of this situation, there is nothing for you to do. Because everything cries, but you cannot help anything. The more you try to help, the more you realize not much helping people. 

For instance, if you are by the deathbed, you try to help. But if you understand the people who are about to die, if you understand the death, very naturally you can accept others’ death. But the moment when you accept others’ death, you see nothing to help. That makes you suffer; that really makes suffering [for you]. But, you have to do it! 

So finally, what you can do is, you should open your life to this deathbed, and to person who is going to die, and to the people who are helping him or her – anyway, all sentient beings; you have to open your life to all things. And then, do it; you can do it. This is *help*. This is help. 

So, I think *shikantaza*, and continue to practice, this kind of thing which seemingly is not worthy of your serious consideration, but it has a very precious, profound meaning. So we have to do it, constantly. Okay? It’s very difficult for you, because it is not the usual way to see in your life.

It’s simple... [Well,] not simple for *you*, but...  *[Laughter, and he laughs.]*

> For example, it is like the time and place of the udumbara flower’s blooming being in fire, during fire. Drilling for fire and flaming fire are both the place and the time of blooming of the udumbara flower. If not for the time and place of the udumbara flower, not a single spark of fire comes into being, there is not a single spark of life. Know that in a single spark of fire are a hundred thousand udumbara flowers, blooming in the sky, blooming on the ground, blooming in the past, blooming in the present. To perceive the time and place of the fire’s appearance is to perceive the udumbara flower. We should not miss perceiving the time and place of the udumbara flower.
> 
> – From *Shōbōgenzō: Zen Essays by Dōgen*, translated by Thomas Cleary.

Okay, here is an example. He says, “For example, it is like the time and place of the udumbara flower’s blooming being in fire, during fire.” 

*[Tape change.]*

... which your life is manifested in the deep sense, possesses great sharp eyes, enough to see the depth of human life – how it is structured, how it is going – and also share the offering the compassion to your life and to others’ life. At that time, every aspect of your life, [like] washing your face, becomes fire. We call it “fire,” we say. So *fire* is not the usual “fire”; fire is energy. It’s really powerful energy of life, to burn up completely. “Burn up” completely means burn up any dualistic perceptions – you know, here and there, a sense of alternative. Anyway, burn up, and create new life. That is *fire*.

So *fire* has two meanings. One is everyday life, every aspect of life. The second is the wisdom fire of *prajñāpāramitā* – wisdom. So, wisdom fire. That is two meanings.

So from this, let’s read “it is like the time and the place of the udumbara flower’s blooming.” So “udumbara flower’s blooming” means, truth is when the time which the truth or absolute or udumbara flowers bloom, in other words *alive*, is in the fire. “In the fire” means in each single aspect of your *gassho*, washing your face, walking on the street, brushing your teeth, et cetera. That is here it says, “it is like the time and place of the udumbara flower’s blooming, being in fire, during the fire.” Constantly during the fire. That is a time of flower blooms. That is the time the precious presence of your life blooms. Where is it? That is a time the absolute is manifested. When you do *gassho*, that is a time, the aspect of your life manifested and simultaneously the absolute or the truth is also manifested there. So both working together as one. That’s why during *gassho*, this activity, human action is very important, and human action is powerful to affect you, to affect others.

So I think that is what Dōgen Zenji says. 

So next, that fire or udumbara shouldn’t be something abstract or metaphysical. It must be concrete aspect of human life. Then he says, 

> Drilling for fire and flaming fire are both the place and the time of blooming of the udumbara flower. 

“Drilling for fire” means in ancient times the primitive people tried to make fire by drilling [a branch]. Do you understand? And that time, you cannot stop drilling until you can get fire. So one of the Zen Master says, “Studying the Way is like drilling for fire: do not cease when you flame fire.” So sometimes we see that the more you practice zazen, you don’t experience particular things for many, many years, that is why you say, “Oh, practicing is ridiculous, so let’s go back to our previous enjoyable life.” That is called ceasing, stop drilling. But that means you find the matches [are] wet. *[Laughter.]* So you say it is impossible to make fire. But if you see the matches are wet, still there is a possibility, because there is a way to dry it up. 

Anyway, even though you see the matches are wet, there is not a reason why you stop it. Constantly you have to go. That is, the Zen Master says, do not cease when you flame fire. Studying the way is like drilling for fire. 

With *[unintelligible]*, we say, what would you say... [practice like your hair is on fire]. Yes – thanks. *[He laughs.]* It is exactly same as this.

“Drilling for fire and the flaming of fire are both the place and the time of blooming of the udumbara flower” – so each aspect of human life is not the usual each aspect of human life, it is something right in the midst of the udumbara flower blooming. So that’s why everyday life is precious, your presence is precious. You have to take best care of it.

> If not for the time and place of the udumbara flower, not a single spark of fire comes into being, there is not a single spark of life. 

This is talking about the enlightenment of Shakyamuni Buddha, by which he saw the star at the dawn on December 8th. “A single spark of fire” means a single spark of star. So if every aspect of human life, including tree’s life and bird’s life, were not right in the midst of the udumbara blooming flower, it wouldn’t be possible to attain enlightenment by seeing the sparkling star at dawn. Because a star is not a star, a star is a being right in the midst of the udumbara flower blooms, the universe. So that’s why if you see the star at the dawn, it gives you something, simultaneously. Beyond whoever you are, you are attained or you are not attained. That’s why Buddha says “all sentient beings attaine simultaneously with me.” The star always gives you, constantly gives universal energy to you. But you are not have enough energy, enough eyes, enough hands to receive it, that’s it; tat’s why we don’t believe it. But actually, in the universally, it’s always giving.

That’s why here it says, “If not for the time and place of the Udumbara flower, not a single spark of fire comes into being...” 

“there is not a single spark of life” – originally it says, “a single spark of fire is not alive.” If everyday life were not Buddha, were not manifestation of the Buddha, it wouldn’t be possible for us to attain enlightenment through everyday living. Through human life. 

So that’s why here it says the everyday life, daily routines, are always alive for us, in the broad sense. 

> Know that in a single spark of fire are a hundred thousand udumbara flowers...

That’s why in a single aspect of everyday life, so called *gassho*, there are many, many thousand udumbara flower blooms. Because if you see the full moon, you know, that is a single aspect of existence. And then how many udumbara flower blooms? Thousand, thousand human beings look at this, and then thousand, thousand human beings are attained by the full moon. In many ways – deeply or narrowly, it doesn’t matter. Anyway communicating the teaching through it. That’s why myriad, myriad, the absolute is manifested there.

So from this point, if you see the tree, tree is trees manifested as a Buddha. So we have to deal with the trees with wholeheartedness. *Gassho*, table... everything. 

So, “Know that in a single spark of fire are a hundred thousand udumbara flowers...”

> ...  blooming in the sky, blooming on the ground, blooming in the past, blooming in the present. 

In the past, in the present, in the future, there are myriad, myriad manifestation of the udumbara flower blooming. That means completely openness, vast openness of the universe. To everyone, to all sentient beings. 

> To perceive the time and place of the fire’s appearance is to perceive the udumbara flower. 

“To perceive the time and place of the fire’s appearance” means to receive the time and place of *gassho*’s appearance, is to receive manifestation of the absolute. Both udumbara flower, fire work together. Not separately. 

That is, he says, “to perceive the time and place of the fire’s appearance is to perceive the udumbara flower.” Well, we don’t do it, because always, if a teacher says deal with a piece of vegetable as a Buddha’s life, and then [we ask,] “How can I see it? How can I see Buddha’s life in a piece of vegetable? Where is it?” Something like this is human speculation, always going like this. But unfortunately or fortunately, I think they’re both working together before you try to analyze. We have to accept both working together, and then you can touch the right code of Life with capital L, which a piece of vegetable possesses. That is our attitude toward kitchen, toward pan, toward the water, toward your life, toward the clothes, toward everything.

> We should not miss perceiving the time and place of the udumbara flower.

That’s why you shouldn’t miss. If you say, “well...” you already miss it. If you say “*yes I do*,” this is already you miss it. If you say “why we have to do this?” Well, it’s missing. But beyond the word, before you create a word, anyway do it, just do it. That’s pretty hard for us, particularly modern human beings, *[he laughs,]* who have lots and lots of words. So it’s very difficult to do it. But anyway, day to day we have to do this.
