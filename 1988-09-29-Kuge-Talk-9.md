#### 0:00

**Katagiri Roshi**: The third paragraph at page 67:

> But it’s not that there are flowers and fruits in spring and autumn; *being time* always has flowers and fruit. Flowers and fruit together preserve time and season, time and season together preserve flowers and fruit. For this reason *the hundred plants* all have flowers and fruits, all trees have flowers and fruits. Gold, silver, copper, iron, coral, and jewel trees all have flowers and fruits; earth, water, fire, air, and space trees all have flowers and fruits. Human trees have flowers, human flowers have flowers, *withered trees* have flowers.
>
> – From *Shōbōgenzō: Zen Essays by Dōgen*, translated by Thomas Cleary.

[From] yesterday, look at the last sentence of the second paragraph:

> Invoking this principle, one can know the measure of spring and autumn.

First of all, we have to invoke this principle. *This principle* means... how can I say it. When the Buddha begins to work, we can do... something. That is called *bodhi-mind*, *practice*, *enlightenment*, *nirvana*, etc. We can do *gassho*, we can come here to do *zazen*, you can attend to training or *sesshin*, et cetera. The reason why you come here is *great activities of Buddha as a cause* – common to human beings and also to all sentient beings. In other words, *buddha* means original energy of Life, with a capital L. But it's not *your* will, actually. Your will is a very small portion of your activities. A *big* portion of the activity or living in this world is something more than your will or volition, or something. So that is called *Buddha*, we say. 

So Buddha starts to work, and Buddha forms fruit. That fruit is called *bodhi-mind*, sometimes. That fruit sometimes is called *attending the sesshin*, or attending the *training session*. That is a fruit. 

So simultaneously in the realm of the original energy of life, I think there is a cause and effect, both work together. Do you understand that one? But usually, [we understand] there is a cause as a flower blooms, and then later it forms fruit. That's natural. But this is the dualistic world we can think [of]. But on the basis of this dualism, there is something... what would you say... vast, great energy there, by which all sentient beings are *caused* to live. Do you understand? *Are caused* to live. Not *you* live; you *are caused* to live. Do you understand that one? This is important.

There are two aspects that work together. You live by your effort. The other aspect is you are caused to live. That means beyond your control, beyond your effort, your existence is exactly there, supported by all sentient beings. That's why naturally you want to do something for something. You cannot always be lazy. 

Most people don't pay attention to the one aspect of human life, who is caused to live by original nature, original energy of life. If you forget this [aspect], you never appreciate [human life], you never show your great deep appreciation to human life – [or] to anyone, to anything. You always complain. You always try to do [things] by your effort, by your [own] powers, by your ability. You really want to do something, but the more you try to do [it] the more you are disappointed, because that is human limitation. 

So, science is advancing endlessly, toward the 21st century. It is. For what? For the sake of creating a better world. It is true. But still there is human limitation, because there is no peace, right in the midst of advancement of science, better world. So *apparently* it seems it may be a happier life, better life, but on the bottom of human life always uneasiness is there. And people notice it, but they don't want to handle it. So they try to cheat their life by doing something exciting. You know, fancy stuff. And then they try to close their eyes. That's it. But uneasiness never [goes] away for those people. So very naturally, there is a human limitation there. 

So we should really pay attention to the human life supported by all sentient beings, buddhas and ancestors, day-to-day. [For two thousand years], they talk about this, but people don't pay attention to it. That is really human arrogance. So we have to pay attention to this. 

That's why first of all, *invoking this principle*: invoking the great original energy of life. 

*How?* Well, why don't you go to sleep with a gassho and chanting? This is a simple activity, but there is a great practice, a great realization of human life, invoking great principle, original energy. That is our practice, day to day. 

So that's why it says, “Invoking this principle, one can know the measure of spring and autumn.” So first of all, then you can see mountains in the spring, you can see mountains in autumn, in summer, in winter. 

That's why yesterday I read [where] Dōgen Zenji says the mountains and waters right now, right here should be seen as the Buddha, the way of Buddhas and ancestors. It means you should see the mountains and the waters as Buddha, not [as] usual objects you are seeing. So for this, you have to really appreciate, you have to really feel the great [breath] of the mountains, and take your breath with the mountains breath. That is making your mind [calm], and face the mountains, and be with it. That is *invoking this principle, one can know the measure of spring and autumn*. 

Then, next paragraph:

> But it’s not that there are flowers and fruits in spring and autumn; ...

In terms of this principle, I think the flowers and fruits, they come into existence only in spring and autumn. But any time, flowers and fruits come into existence. That's why next here it says:

> ... *being time* always has flowers and fruit. 

*Being time* means no beginning and no end of past, present, future. It means *eternity*. *Eternity* means original great energy of life, common to all living beings. This is *being time*, eternity. 

So where is the eternity? [When] you are eternity, nothing to count, nothing to calculate, nothing to go back, nothing to be, nothing to stay a certain place. From moment to moment you must be *in a* moment. That is *one*; only one. 

So eternity is what? Where is the eternity? Eternity is *in one*. “In one” means, when you do *gassho*, there is eternity. The moment is eternity; eternity is the moment. 

[The] circle is a symbol of the whole universe in Zen; drawing a circle is the truth of the whole universe. But this circle consists of countless number of dots. So, each dot is one. So, countlessly, countlessly there are dots. How can you count? No way. You can count for a certain period of time, [but] you simply cannot continue to count, because [it’s] countless. So, finally, you should give up; *you should give up* means you should give your life to that countless number, and then be with that countless number. That's it. 

What do I mean? It means it is just like being in the huge ocean, thrown over the boat. Nothing there, but your head spins very quickly, seeing, imagining the big boat, the wonderful person, the saviors and the wonderful food and many things. You can think of it, but whatever you think, what is it? Where you are, just one. *Just one* is what? Eternal, huge ocean. Where is the huge ocean? Just here. 

What can I do? Can I think many beings and where they are existing, in the big oceans? Yes you can – but it doesn't make sense. So, finally what you can do is, offer your body and mind to the vastness of oceans. Then be there, and do something; work on your life. That's it.

Then when you get to the big boat to save yourself, you are a lucky guy. *[He laughs.]* But maybe not, maybe not. At that time, you have to die. Okay? You can die; I don't know; there is no guarantee whether you will be able to die peacefully or not, because I have never experienced it. But I can tell you, there is nothing to do except death, when you have to die. So, you have to die. 

But you cannot imagine death, because you are right in the spot. Right [where]? One. Just [the] number one. But just [the] number one is, where is it? It's a huge world. So, lots of possibilities there. 

So that is eternity. Okay? Eternity, *being time* – no end, no beginning of past, present, future. This is the original energy of life. 

You shouldn't forget it. You have to know it; you have to know, you have to learn through the scriptures and ancestors' teachings. But the main point is, you have to *actualize* it through the practice. That is simple practice. That is really simple practice. 

That is *being time*. That's why *being time* in the realm of *no end, no beginning of past, present, future*, always there is flower and fruit, because nothing to complain, nothing to comment on your presence in the ocean, nothing to say. But there is a cause, there is a fruit. Cause is what? Cause is just being there. [And] the fruit is what? Just being there. That's it. 

All you can do is, from the beginning to end, being there, being there. So, that is a fruit and cause, fruit and cause. What makes you do in that way? That is Buddha; the original energy of life calls you to act in that way. That's it. Whatever your head thinks, completely beyond your head, your activity moves, goes like that. 

That is what Dōgen Zenji says here: “Being time always has flowers and fruit.”

#### 18:50

> Flowers and fruit together preserve time and season, time and season together preserve flowers and fruit. 

So there is simultaneously flowers and fruit, and that flowers and fruit preserve time and season. *Preserve* doesn't mean to carry the two things mixed. Do you understand? *Preserve* doesn't mean to carry two things, together. *Preserve* here means flowers and fruit *are* simultaneously time and season. 

According to my example, being on the ocean: you can see it. Be calm, quiet, and see that situation; exactly there is a fruit and flowers. The flower of presence is there, [life is] there. Simultaneously, what is it? No commentary. It is a fruit. Simultaneously, fruit is your life. The cause and the effect there. That is what? Time. Just time. What is time? Time is just arising. Just arising. That arising doesn't leave anything behind *arising*; [it’s] just pure activity called arising. 

If you read *Being Time* in this book, you can see it: the very pure energies coming up. That is the original energy of life everyone has, all sentient beings have. 

That is “flowers and fruit together preserve time and season, time and season together preserve flowers and fruit.”

That's why when you do *gassho*, anyway you have to do *gassho*. This is a time and season you [cannot] avoid. So you have to do it; and then there is a fruit and flowers. Then the flowers and the fruit, time and season, work and support your life. That preserves your life, all sentient beings, and nurture. 

> For this reason *the hundred plants* all have flowers and fruits, all trees have flowers and fruits. 

So for this reason. That's why *the hundred plants* means all things, all forms, all marks of existence. Trees, insects, and air, and sky, and stars, and all things the world holds. This is, here it says, all plants. The hundred millions, billions of plants all have flowers and fruit. That's why their lives are precious. Your life is precious, because there is already fruits and flowers. 

And also flowers and fruits are nurtured by time and season simultaneously before you think it. That is sometimes we call *before your birth*. So life is going on before your birth means before you think of it, before you poke your head into it. That is your real life. So all trees have flowers and fruit. So from this point, 

> Gold, silver, copper, iron, coral, and jewel trees all have flowers and fruits; earth, water, fire, air, and space trees all have flowers and fruits. Human trees have flowers, human flowers have flowers, *withered trees* have flowers.

Dōgen Zenji brings up many names, but myriad billions of beings exist you can see through your six senses, so we cannot bring up all things. But it means *anything* which you can see, hear, or think, or touch with your body, or feel, whatever it is. All things have flowers and fruit. 

That is what is says here. Even the withered trees, dead trees have anyway flowers and trees. 

That’s why in the *Shōbōgenzō* there is a special chapter called “Withered Tree and Dragon’s Roar,” something like that. So a dead tree is not dead, which means useless; this is human speculation. But a dead tree is already a dead tree which has flowers and fruit. So it is precious. That’s why woodpeckers always live there! *[He laughs.]* *[Laughter.]*

So, anyway, nothing to [waste] in this world. You know in this chapter “Flowers in the Sky” means the point of this chapter is to teach us that all forms or [marks] you can experience through your six senses are precious. Precious; *Buddha*, we say. 

So that is what Dōgen Zenji tries to say. But intellectually we are separated. We are Buddha, but we are not Buddha, so we try to practice and let’s get the Buddha. That’s the usual sense. But that is not an appropriate understanding of Buddha’s teaching; that’s why Dōgen Zenji corrects our understanding, and trains us to see human world from a completely different way called Buddha’s way. 

I think the translator also says in the commentary, page 65, the last sentence, he says:

> ... this essay by Dogen is aimed at cutting through the notion of nirvana as opposite to mundane life.

So we are living in the mundane world. That’s why the mundane world is just like a bubble, just like a dream, just like a hallucination – perverted views. But this is the Buddha’s expedient method, okay? It doesn’t mean the world we live in is a dream. It’s not a dream; it’s Buddha’s world. “We are deluded”: this is the Buddha’s expedient method. But actually, we are Buddha. We are Buddha. But we don’t believe [it]; that’s why naturally we see “Buddha, we are deluded.” So that’s why, “let’s remove deluded mind and then let’s get undeluded mind.” This is a common [belief]. But if you practice based on this ideas, it’s very difficult for you to carry on practice, in peace, in harmony. Even though your practice is advancing, right in the middle of advancement there is no peace. 

The same applies to the sciences. Do you understand? If you practice Buddhism in the common sense: “Let’s get enlightenment.” *[He laughs.]* So day to day you can experience wonderful things, sometimes. Most days you can experience [this] because you have lots of energies to get into it. So very naturally, you feel happy, because you can see the great advancement of your life, day to day. Okay? But *I don’t know*... whether there is peace, real peace, repose and bliss, right in the process of advancement. Usually advancement is not always advancing – *advanceable*, useful. Sometimes it disturbs you; sometimes it irritates you. Don’t you think so? 

For instance, look at your life. Sometimes people want to become a priest, and then I say, “No, not yet.” And then they are disappointed. And then they try to become a priest, and the moment when you become a priest, you already feel, “I am a great, advanced student,” something like that. *[He laughs.]* And then the people invite you and you want to go someplace to teach. And then that event makes you more, you know, creating arrogance, “I am advanced, advanced, advanced.” Naturally, of course, in this situation you can see the advancement of your life as a priest, of course. But I don’t know whether there is really bliss and peace, repose and bliss, in the process of this kind of advancement. 

Religion. Buddhism. Buddhas and ancestors constantly give you what is bliss and repose, perfect peace. What is it? It is in the process of advancement. It must be in the process of advancement. Or, even though you are poor, even though your life doesn’t advance, there must be peace. That is important. 

But people are very greedy to get advancement quickly. Because it’s clear to get a certain fruit called advancement. But you completely forget real peace, right there. So that’s why you become more greedy, and greediness creates more arrogance. And arrogance creates more awful desires, and miserable desires to put the people down, and criticizing to the world. That is what always happens. So you must be careful. 

So that’s why, *in every aspect of human life there is fruit and flower* is very important. It is not the cause of creating arrogance or the advancement people really expect. It’s not. What is Buddha? Our life is Buddha. What does it mean? We have to really pay attention to it. 

#### 33:16

So, next:

> Among such as these existing, there are the *flowers in the sky* spoken of by the Buddha. 

The *flowers in the sky spoken of by the Buddha* is not the kind of perverted view which the people who have kind of cataracts see. So what are the flowers in the sky spoken of by the Buddha? That is what Dōgen Zenji he tries to say in this paragraph. 

> However, those of little learning and little insight do not know what the colors, luster, leaves and blossoms of the *flowers in the sky* are like – they only hear of them as nonexistent flowers. Know that in the Buddha Way there is talk of *flowers in the sky* – ...

For instance, if you do zazen, you can see the lights. Can’t you? You can see sometimes light, colorful lights right in front of your eyes. If you look at the world in little tiny spot, creating the gorgeous, you know, Avalokiteshvara’s look. Sometimes animals, sometimes ghosts appear. Have you ever experienced? *[He chuckled.]* I experienced [it] a lot. *[Laughter.]* That is kind of flowers in the sky, we say. *[He laughs.]* So, it’s not good. 

Or, in the usual [sense] *flowers in the sky* means to see the various aspects of human life as delusions or dreams, or perverse. At that time, all forms of human life are called *flowers in the sky*, usually. So it’s not good. 

Then, we say by the practice, by the zazen, let’s remove these clouds around the sun and the moon. And then – all gone. At that time you can see the moon and the sun. That is the usual sense; we talk about the Buddha’s teaching like that. But Dōgen Zenji doesn’t say it in that way. Because clouds are also what? Flowers in the sky; it means great energies, great original energy of life. Without clouds, how can we exist? So clouds are important for us.

 And seeing the lights during zazen: what’s wrong [with it]? This is also part of your experience. But it’s not necessary to stick to it, or not necessary to ignore it, because you can see it. So you have to deal with it. Anyway, you have to deal with it. 
 
So if it interrupts you a little bit, you have to deal with them in a different way. If you feel good, you can deal with it in a different way. But whatever happens, it’s not necessary to cling to it, it’s not necessary to leave away. 

So be with it, and work on it. That’s always what we do. 

So whatever you do, not only the flowers in the sky, so-called deluded imagination, deluded image, deluded lights – but enlightenment is also a deluded image! If you think so. If you think the human world is deluded in terms of the idea of flowers in the sky, even the enlightenment, even the good activities are all deluded. But we are living there, so we cannot escape from [it]. So that’s why the samsaric world is very precious for us. 

So, “Among such as these existing, there are the *flowers in the sky* spoken of by the Buddha. However, those of little learning and little insight do not know what the colors, luster, leaves and blossoms of the *flowers in the sky* are like – they only hear of them as nonexistent flowers. Know that in the Buddha Way there is talk of *flowers in the sky* – “...

> ... outsiders don’t know the talk about *flowers in the sky*, much less consciously understand it. Only Buddhas and Zen adepts alone know the blooming and falling of sky flowers and earth flowers, ...

“Sky flowers and earth flowers” means sky itself is magnificently precious. Because it has a fruit and flower simultaneously. Which means the sky has great energies, powers to *be*, qualified to be, to exist; everyone. So that’s why sky flowers, earth flowers; that’s why we say “Mother Earth.” 

> ... know the blooming and falling of world flowers, ...

So everything has flowers and fruit, which are precious. 

> ... and so on, and know the sky flowers, earth flowers, world flowers, and so on are scriptures. 

That is a kind of talking about the Buddha’s teachings. Beautiful scriptures, beautiful panoramic pictures of Buddha’s teachings. You can see that on the scroll, something like that. Because all sentient beings come together;

*[tape break?]*

Dōgen Zenji says, “In order to fulfill this number of members, all things are assembled and made grandiose.” So making all things come together and create wonderful world. 

> This is the guideline for Buddhist study. 

So this is the guideline. If you forget this guideline, it’s very difficult for you to touch the core, the real meaning of Buddha’s teachings. So even if you don’t understand it, or you understand it, anyway we have to move toward this practice. Because this is the guideline for human beings. “Buddhist” [here] means human beings. 

Flowers, fruit; that is the guideline. 

> Because that which Buddhas and Zen adepts ride on is *flowers in the sky*, the worlds of Buddhas as well as the teachings of the Buddhas are in fact *flowers in the sky*.

Next, page 68. 

> However, what ordinary ignoramuses...

“Ignoramuses”? *[Laughter.]* Is that okay? 

**Audience**: Yes.

**Katagiri Roshi:** Have you ever heard this one? *[Laughter.]*

**Someone:** My mother used to *always* use it. 

**Katagiri Roshi:** Is that right? *[He laughs.]* *[Laughter.]*

It’s a very popular word? “Old style”?

**Someone:** It’s a mother word. *[Laughter.]*

**Someone:** Sometimes it has a critical connotation. 

**Katagiri Roshi:** I know that. *[Laughter.]*

**Someone:** Rather negative. 

**Katagiri Roshi:** Negative, huh? *[He laughs.]*

> However, what ordinary ignoramuses think when they hear that the Buddha said what eyes with cataracts see are *flowers in the sky*, ...

This is the usual understanding, okay? 

> ... is that *eyes with cataracts* refers to the distorted eyes of sentient beings; ...

Do you understand that one? That’s why all [the] world is deluded, perverted views, or dreams. 

> ... they reason that since diseased eyes are distorted, they perceive nonexistent flowers in the clear sky. By clinging to this reasoning, they think it means falsely seeing as existent the nonexistent three worlds, six paths, existent Buddhas, nonexistent Buddhas. 

That’s always what the Buddhist scripture says, you know? Do you understand? We cling to this table because I like it, you know? But Buddhist scripture says it’s not real; it’s not [a real thing] you can cling or you can depend on, because it’s empty, it’s moving, it’s *impermanent*, something like that. But if you cling to it, it becomes delusion. That’s what usually we say. That is called *flowers in the sky* in the usual sense. 

> They make a living on the idea that this says that if the deluding cataracts in the eyes were gone, these flowers in the sky would not be seen, and so there are originally no flowers in the sky. 

Then if you remove those deluded thinking or views, then, all gone. 

*[Tape break.]*

> It is a pity that people like this don’t know the time and season and process of the *flowers in the sky* spoken of by Buddha. The principle of the *cataracts in the eye*, *flowers in the sky* spoken of by Buddhas is not yet apparent to ordinary people and outsiders. The Buddhas, enlightened ones, cultivating these *flowers in the sky*, thereby *acquire the robe, seat, and room*, attain enlightenment and realize its fruition. *Holding up a flower and winking* is a public case where *cataracts in the eye*, *flowers in the sky* manifest. 

In this case, I think in terms of Buddha’s eyes, *flowers in the sky* is also the scenery which Buddha showed, udumbara flowers in the front of his assembly. Look at the notes: first, note eight. 

> 8. The Zen legend of the beginning of the Zen transmission is that once before an assembly Shakyamuni Buddha said nothing but held up a flower and winked – no one understood but the chief disciple Mahā-Kāśyapa, who smiled. Buddha said, “I have the treasury of the eye of true teaching, the ineffable mind of nirvana, the true form, which is formless – this I hand on to Mahā-Kāśyapa.”

I mentioned this before. This is a story. [Dōgen Zenji] says here, “*Holding up a flower and winking* is a public case where *cataracts in the eye, flowers in the sky* manifest. This is also *flowers in the sky* manifest. 

And see number seven: 

> 7. “Robe, seat, and room” is an expression from the *Hokke* scripture: the “robe” of Buddhas is conciliation and tolerance; the ‘‘seat’’ of Buddhas is the emptiness of all things; the “room” of Buddhas is compassion for all beings.

The *Hokke* scripture is the *Lotus Sutra*. I think in the chapter, I forgot the number, but “The Teacher,” I think in the *Saddharma Pundarika Sutra* in “The Teacher,” Buddha Shakyamuni talked to the monks who were about to preach, and he said to all of them like this: “Before you talk, before you preach, you have to wear the robe as conciliation and tolerance” – that means patience. *Conciliation and tolerance* means *patience*. You have to wear the robe; this is the symbol of Buddha’s robe, patience. 

And the seat of Buddha: you have to sit on the Buddha’s seat. It’s not on a high platform which is fancy, *[he laughs]*, which a [king] or princes sit on. You have to sit on the seat of the Buddha, which means *emptiness* of all beings. There is no particular thing you can claim, you can think, you can imagine. You have to just be there. Just *be* there. 

And next, you have to be in the room of the Buddha. Not in the room of human society, but you have to be in the room of Buddha. It means you have to be in the room of great energy of life. 

At that time, then you can teach. That’s what the Buddha says, there. 

So if you do like this, very naturally you can practice at least three [things]. One is you can experience, you can [do the] practice of *nothing to be carried away by praise*. Second, by... what is the opposite of praise? Blame. Yes. Criticism. Third, you can be in peace and harmony right in the midst of human life, surrounded by fame and power, up and down, happy or unhappy. 

At least, you can experience this. In other words, *experience* means you can cultivate this practice. If you always before you teach you have to sit, you have to wear the robe, that means you have to wear the robe of patience; conciliation and also tolerance. And then you have to be in the emptiness. And you have to get into the room of Buddha; that means the big scale of human existence. And then very naturally, you can just sit down and start to preach. At that time, whatever happens after that, or right in the middle, well, you can just continue to do it. 

One day several years ago, I went to the University of Minnesota and talked about Zen Buddhism. There was an old Japanese woman, who teaches Japanese at the University. So I started talking, and in the last of my talk I said, “I don’t care about how poor my English was.” *[He laughs.]* I looked immediately at the old Japanese lady, and she immediately made a face, “Mmm-mm-mm-mm.” *[He laughs.]* Because she teaches Japanese language, anyway. But I don’t mean [it that way]; “I don’t care” doesn’t mean I don’t care. *I must be there*, but I mustn’t be carried away by poor and good or bad. 

Well, I’m not proud of my English, *[he laughs,]* and I’m not proud of my Japanese language either, you know? So whatever kind of language I use, it’s pretty difficult to communicate with people. When I went to Nebraska and talked Buddhism at the University [of Nebraska] at Kearney, one young gentleman criticized me. *[He laughs.]* He used a very rough word: “Hey, teacher, do believe you can communicate with Americans with your English?” *[He laughs.]* I said, “No.” I said, “Yes.” I said took both answers: “Yes” or “No.” And he said, “Do you believe you can communicate with your language?” I said, “No.” “Can you believe no communication with Americans?” “Yes, I do.” *[He laughs.]* And then I explained: “Can you communicate with people with your language? You are an English-speaking person; how much can you communicate with people? The more language is easy to use for you as a mother language, you make lots of mistakes! You misuse the language. The more you use the language, the more complicated, the more you create a gap. So finally, well, you don’t know what the language is. So you have to stop talking, and then just be together. Be there and shake hands; that’s it. That’s all we can do, finally.” Much less I use a different language. I don’t believe I can completely communicate with the people. 

So he kept quiet. *[He laughs.]* 

It’s very interesting, if I go here and there: many different beings there, you know? Many different beings, flowers in the sky. Different fruits, different flowers. 

So, that’s what it says here.
