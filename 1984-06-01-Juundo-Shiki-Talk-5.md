Or *bendō*. *Ben* is with a sense of completion you have to be in the Way (*dō*). In other words, be one with the universe. That is constantly you should do it. This is *bendō*. When *bendō* is expressed from a different angle, at that time it is called *kufu*. So *kufu* and *bendō* are almost the same meaning.

Do you remember the story in [...] Dogen Zenji [...] in China? In *Shobogenzo Zuimonki* he said it like this: 

> When I read an ancestor’s analects while in the monastery in China, a monk from Seisen asked me as follows: “What do you read the ancestor’s analects for?”
>
> – From *Shōbōgenzō-zuimonki* section 2-9; probably Katagiri Roshi’s own translation.

[Seisen] is the name of the place; a monk [from] Seisen (probably Shisen or Sichuan) who was also practicing at Tendo-ji when Dogen Zenji was there.

So Dogen Zenji said, “Because I want to understand and know the ancestors’ lives.”

And the monk said, “What do you want to know their lives for?”
 
 And Dogen then said, “Because I will want to help the people when I go back to Japan.” 

And then the monk said, “What for?” 

And Dogen Zenji said, “Because I want to benefit their lives.” 

The monk said, “Ultimately, what for?” 

And Dogen Zenji reflected upon himself; at that time, he was completely speechless. 

So this is a very interesting story. Even though you teach, you give the words to the people, if the words are not digested by your life, sooner or later you become speechless. When the people ask you constantly, you don’t know what it is. 

In other words if you borrow the words from the scripture and teach, then sooner or later you become speechless, because you don’t know. That words and teaching you have given is something borrowed from others, from the teaching. So it’s not your story, that’s the teaching’s story. 

So that’s why, first of all, the most important point is, the one person [...] who exists right now, right here – lively, lively, exactly lively. We need this kind of person. And then very naturally this kind of person can get the words from his or her heart. That is great. Then, at that time, you can really help. 

So this is the most important point Dogen Zenji talks about in this paragraph.
